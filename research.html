Short-Range Correlations

Most nucleons in a nucleus move independently in the average field of their neighbours. Now and then two of them come close enough to overlap and form a short-range correlated pair, with a relative momentum above the Fermi momentum, kF ≈ 250 MeV/c, and a small centre-of-mass momentum.

We break such pairs apart and detect both nucleons. The mix of pn, pp and nn pairs, and how it changes with momentum, maps the nucleon–nucleon interaction at short distance. Neutron–proton pairs dominate around 400 MeV/c, where the tensor force rules (PRL 113, 022501). Towards 800 MeV/c that dominance fades and the ratio of proton–proton to neutron–proton pairs rises, as expected when the tensor force gives way to an isospin-independent repulsive core (PLB 820, 136523).

We also ask which nucleons pair up in the first place. Recent data point to nuclear shell structure (Nature 654, 619; arXiv:2606.07754), and we model pair abundances with shell-model wave functions (PRC 114, 034002).

Nucleons in the Nuclear Medium

A nucleon bound in a nucleus is not quite a free nucleon. Deep inelastic scattering shows that its quark distributions are modified, the EMC effect, and decades later the origin of the effect is still debated. One leading idea ties it to short-range correlated pairs: brief, dense fluctuations in which the internal structure of the nucleons changes (RMP 89, 045002; Nature 566, 354).

We test this with tagged deep inelastic scattering on deuterium, where a backward-going spectator nucleon reveals, event by event, how deeply bound the struck nucleon was. Gluons come next: their behaviour at large x inside nuclei is almost unknown, and an approved Jefferson Lab experiment, with Igor among its spokespersons, will probe it through J/ψ photoproduction near threshold (arXiv:2510.22076).

The Nucleon in Three Dimensions

Elastic scattering shows where the charge sits inside a nucleon, and deep inelastic scattering shows how its quarks share its momentum. Deeply virtual exclusive processes, in which the electron produces a real photon or a meson and leaves the nucleon intact, connect the two through generalized parton distributions, which relate the longitudinal momentum of a quark to its position in the transverse plane.

Our work here includes deeply virtual Compton scattering measurements, among them one on the neutron (Nature Phys. 16, 191), and a reanalysis of published CLAS π0 data that separates the longitudinal and transverse cross sections, a test of the factorization on which this picture rests (arXiv:2103.00611).

The Electron-Ion Collider

The Electron-Ion Collider at Brookhaven National Laboratory will collide electrons with protons and nuclei to image the quarks and gluons inside them. For our physics, a collider changes the game: spectators and recoiling nucleons, which are slow and hard to catch in a fixed target, emerge boosted to high momentum (PRC 105, 034001).

We take part in designing the detectors and the measurements, starting with the ECCE detector concept (NIM A 1073, 170240) and its exclusive, diffractive and tagging programme (NIM A 1052, 168238).

Experiments at Jefferson Lab

Jefferson Lab's CEBAF accelerator delivers electron beams of up to 11 GeV to Halls A, B and C, and 12 GeV to Hall D. Our experiments span all four.

Our experiments include:

  • Hall A, 4He(e,e′pN). Triple-coincidence measurement of pn and pp pairs at missing momenta of 400–830 MeV/c, where the nucleon–nucleon force turns from tensor to repulsive (PRL 113, 022501).
  • Hall B (CLAS), 12C(e,e′pN), A(e,e′p). Pair break-up up to 1000 MeV/c, across the tensor-to-scalar transition, and cross-section ratios that show correlated pairs taking over above roughly 350 MeV/c (PLB 820, 136523; PRC 107, L061301).
  • Hall B (CLAS12 + BAND), d(e,e′ns)X. The Backward Angle Neutron Detector tags spectator neutrons emitted at 155°–175° with momenta of 200–600 MeV/c (NIM A 978, 164356).
  • Hall C (SHMS + HMS), A(e,e′p). High-missing-momentum protons knocked out of nuclei from 9Be to 197Au with a 10.5 GeV beam, to learn how pairing depends on mass, neutron excess and shell structure (arXiv:2606.07754).
  • Hall C (LAD), d(e,e′ps)X. The Large Acceptance Detector tags backward-going spectator protons, to measure how the structure of a high-momentum, highly virtual neutron is modified (EPJA 60, 201).
  • Hall D (GlueX), γ 4He → J/ψ X. Approved experiment, with Igor among its spokespersons, to measure J/ψ photoproduction at and below threshold: a first stringent test of how gluons behave inside bound nucleons (E12-25-002).
